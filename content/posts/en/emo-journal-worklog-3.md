<br>It felt a bit sudden, but as I kept doing it little by little, the time for QA arrived before I knew it. To be honest, I feel like I&#39;m way too late because I was hoping for a stroke of luck. Sometimes, just getting things done quickly seems to be enough, but anyway, here I am again, neglecting my own performance and taking care of myself the most. The best thing about exercise and work is that there is an end to it, but working alone never feels like there is, so it is always difficult. The end? Is it? Where is it? Still, I should have set a finish line for myself and rewarded myself with at least a cookie, but I just ended up whipping myself every time. Anyway, I proceed with the QA. I made up the list, but I hated doing it so much that I opened my blog, screamed loudly on silent mode, and scrolled through noisy Euljiro for a long time. The list came out to about 60 items, and once I actually started, most of them passed—rather anticlimatically. Since I performed simple case tests at the functional level and moved on, there were no major issues. As expected, the hardest part of going this far was the thought &quot;So what... what is this supposed to do?&quot; that crossed my mind far too often. No matter how much I tried to reassure myself that I was just giving it a try, it happened every time. I’ve fixed some minor things, and now I plan to have Codex report lexicon false positive risks, clean up unused immorts, clean up consol.log/consol.error, and add accessibilityLables. After that, I’ll convert it to a web format, share it with you guys, and get feedback... It would be nice to get a job in the meantime.
